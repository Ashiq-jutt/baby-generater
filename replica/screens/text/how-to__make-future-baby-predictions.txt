Prepare a pair that is easy to compare
One face in each file Choose a front-facing portrait with both eyes, the nose, lips and jaw visible. If you only have a couple photo, make two separate crops with one person in each. Keep enough resolution to see the face; cutting a tiny face out of a distant group photo will not restore detail.
Similar framing and light Crop both photos from the head to the shoulders at a similar scale. Soft, even light makes the visible features easier to read. Avoid pairing a strongly filtered selfie with an unfiltered photo, or a dark side profile with a bright frontal view.
One parent photo is also supported The quick steps describe the two-photo route. The current homepage also accepts one parent photo, which produces a creative interpretation from that one input. Leave the second slot empty instead of uploading the same person twice to imitate a pair.
Choose a variation, then generate a still image
For a fair comparison, keep the same source pair and change only Boy/Girl or one source crop. Save the files locally so you can select them again if the upload fields reset after submission. A second generation is a separate attempt and may use more credits.
Add your own files Open the homepage generator and use Parent Photo 1 and Parent Photo 2. The faces initially shown in empty slots are illustrations, not files already selected for you. Use JPG, PNG or WebP and check the previews before continuing.
Boy, Girl or Surprise Me Choose the kind of portrait you want. Surprise Me leaves that creative choice to the generator; it does not predict a pregnancy’s sex. The current homepage has no twins option, age selector or parent-resemblance percentage slider.
Review the credit cost Sign in, check the displayed cost and submit once. The current workflow creates a square image with a 1K setting; there is no model or aspect-ratio picker on this form. Wait for the result status before starting another attempt.

Judge the picture before trying another version
One source seems to dominate Check whether the other photo is darker, smaller or more angled. Replace only that weaker input and compare again. The generator does not provide a measured genetic blend, so a result that feels uneven is not a percentage you can verify.
Eyes, teeth or hair look odd Open the result at full size. Inspect eye alignment, the smile and hair edges. Try a sharper original with fewer obstructions. Enlarging a blurry source does not recover missing facial detail.
Each result looks different Keep the same two files and note the selected option. Pick a portrait you like for its coherent face and composition, not because repeated attempts make a biological prediction more certain.

FAQ
Do I need high-resolution photos? ▼ Higher resolution helps, but consistent lighting and a clear face matter most.
Why does the result look unrealistic? ▼ Try clearer parent photos with similar angles and lighting. Avoid strong filters and heavy makeup in the inputs.
Can I try multiple times? ▼ Yes—small input changes (crop/lighting) often improve results.
Is this a scientific prediction? ▼ No. The tool creates an imagined portrait from the two photos. It does not analyze DNA or predict a future child’s appearance.
Can I use one couple photo instead of two portraits? ▼ For the clearest inputs, crop it into two files with one face in each and upload them separately. If either face is very small or obscured, choose a closer photo instead. The homepage also accepts one individual parent photo.
Can I choose twins, an exact age or a 50/50 resemblance? ▼ Those controls are not on the current homepage. It offers Boy, Girl and Surprise Me. The portraits are creative outputs, not a measurable inheritance calculation. Age progression is a separate tool.
Does a realistic result show how my actual child will look? ▼ No. Realistic rendering is an image style, not evidence of genetic accuracy. The tool does not analyze DNA, establish parentage or predict a future child’s health or appearance.
