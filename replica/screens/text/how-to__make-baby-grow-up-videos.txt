Choose a photo that gives the model a clear face
Check the source before generating a whole sequence. If the eyes or mouth are unclear in the original, a larger exported image will not recover those details.
One face, facing the camera
Use a photo with both eyes visible. Crop other people out and avoid a hand, pacifier or toy covering the mouth; those areas otherwise have to be invented.
Soft light and visible detail
Choose an evenly lit photo where you can inspect the eyes at full size. Avoid strong shadows, blur and beauty filters that erase the features you want to compare.
Room around the head
Include the whole head and some shoulders. A crop tight against the hairline leaves little room to match the framing across older portraits.
A simple background
A plain wall makes changes easier to spot. A detailed room or busy outdoor scene can change between ages, so plan to crop or accept a scene cut.
Upload JPG, PNG or WebP, up to 10 MB. Use the original photo file when possible rather than a screenshot of a social post.

3 Set the timing
Place the original at 0–2 seconds, age 5 at 2–4 seconds and age 18 at 4–6 seconds. Play once with no transition. If you add a short dissolve, recheck the total duration and look for a distracting double face.
4 Add labels and optional sound
Use short labels such as “Original”, “Age 5 · AI” and “Age 18 · AI”. Keep them below the face and away from the edges. Add a voice message or music you have permission to use, then listen through the cuts.
5 Export and watch the saved file
Where your editor supports it, MP4 at 1080p and 30 fps is a practical starting point. Exporting at a higher resolution does not add missing facial detail. Open the saved video on your phone and check the crop, labels, first frame, last frame and sound.

When the result does not cut together well
Find the problem in the still images first. A transition cannot repair a face you already dislike.
The older portrait looks like someone else Compare the eyes, eyebrows, nose and smile with the original. Retry the affected age from the original photo; if the source is blurry or filtered, replace it before generating more ages.
The head jumps at every cut Align the eye line and match the head size in the editor. If aligning one frame requires cutting off the hair or chin, use a wider crop or generate that frame again.
Clothes or backgrounds suddenly change This can happen even with the same settings, as the age-5 example shows. Keep straight cuts for a montage, crop distracting areas, or choose a closer visual match. A long dissolve can make the mismatch more obvious.
Two faces overlap during a transition Shorten or remove the dissolve and check alignment. A dissolve fades between two images; it does not generate the missing ages or turn the face smoothly into the next one.
The video feels rushed or the labels get clipped Extend the still-image duration in the editor and give every age time to register. Preview on the device you will share to; move text inward and inspect the crop before exporting again.
Tips
Avoid heavy filters and strong makeup in the input photo.
Front-facing photos usually preserve identity better.
Try multiple ages—some age targets look more natural than others.
FAQ
Why does it look like a different person? ▼ Identity drift often happens with low-quality or heavily filtered photos. Use a sharper photo with natural lighting.
Which age should I choose first? ▼ Try a mid-range age like 10 or 50 first, then explore younger/older options.
Can I use non-baby photos? ▼ You can, but results are usually best when the subject is a baby in the input photo.
Does Baby Grow Up export a finished growth video? ▼ The Baby Grow Up tool generates still portraits at selected ages. Download the images and arrange them in a video editor. Optional image-to-video generation is a separate step, with its own settings and credit cost.
How many images do I need for a short video? ▼ The worked edit uses three: the original plus two generated ages. At two seconds per image with straight cuts, it lasts six seconds. Add more ages only if you want a longer sequence.
Can I use real photos from different years? ▼ Yes. Put actual family photos in chronological order in your video editor and align the faces. You do not need age progression for years you already have photographs of. Label generated ages if you mix them with real photos.
Will the generated adult look like my child in the future? ▼ There is no way to verify that from this tool. It creates an imagined appearance from a photo and a requested age; it is not a genetic or medical prediction.
