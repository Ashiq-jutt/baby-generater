AI Baby Dance Video
A dancing baby video needs two inputs: a baby photo for the character and a dance reference video for the movement. The current output is vertical (9:16), and its duration follows the reference clip.
Choose one dance move, then give it a character. The reference video supplies the movement; the photo supplies the baby’s appearance. This guide walks through trimming that reference, matching the photo and checking the finished dance.

Trim one complete move before uploading
Find a readable sequence Use one visible dancer and a steady camera. A short sway, bounce or step is easier to compare than a montage. Avoid cuts, people crossing in front of the dancer and movements that leave the frame.
Set the beginning and ending In a video editor, trim to a complete movement with a little room before and after it. Start with 5–10 seconds as a practical test, not a required tool setting. Keep the file within 30 seconds and 50 MB, then watch the exported reference once.
Match the photo to the move For steps, show the feet, knees, arms and space around the body. For a close portrait, choose a small upper-body motion. A seated or tightly wrapped baby is a poor match for a standing dance. Use one clear subject for your first attempt.

Choose settings after checking your inputs
Kling V2.6 or V3 Motion Control Both expose Standard/Pro and an optional video prompt. Begin with the default model and a short reference; compare another model using the same inputs. Pro is a quality option, not a repair for missing feet or an obstructed dancer.
DreamActor This alternative uses the same photo-and-motion task, but does not show the Kling mode and prompt controls. Check the current estimate when switching models; do not assume they cost the same.
Duration, framing and upload The editor detects duration from the reference and rounds it up for the estimate. There is no separate duration or aspect-ratio selector. Prepare vertical framing before upload. If a MOV file will not preview, export an MP4 with H.264 video and recheck its size and length.
Optional scene direction for Kling
Keep the red outfit and Christmas-tree background consistent. Steady camera and soft indoor light. Use this only when it matches your photo. The reference still controls the choreography; a longer prompt cannot replace the motion video.

Review the dance at full speed, then pause
Download a usable take before testing another. For a loop, choose nearby start and end poses in your editor and watch the join several times; automatic generation does not guarantee a seamless loop.
Feet slide or legs cross awkwardly Pause when a foot should be planted. Try a reference with smaller steps and a photo showing both legs. Keep the photo fixed while testing the simpler motion so you can see what changed.
Face, hands or clothing drift Inspect turns and moments when hands cross the face. Shorten the reference around an unobstructed move, or use a clearer front-facing photo. Check the beginning, middle and end, not just the poster frame.
Music and the final crop Kling requests the reference’s original sound, so listen to the downloaded file before adding music. Trim and adjust audio in a video editor; avoid stacking two copies of the same track. Keep feet and raised hands inside the final crop, and preview once on a phone.

Tips
If motion looks weird, the fix is usually a better photo and crop—not a longer prompt.
Keep the face centered and large in frame for fewer artifacts.
Shorter clips look cleaner and loop better.
FAQ
Why does the dance motion look weird? ▼ Check both inputs. A reference with cuts, fast turns or hidden limbs can cause motion errors. Try a shorter, continuous shot and a photo with the body visible.
Should I use 9:16 or 1:1? ▼ The current Dancing Baby tool produces vertical 9:16 output. It has no 1:1 selector. If you need a square post, crop the downloaded video in a separate editor.
How do I make it more viral? ▼ Use a strong hook in the first second (big face, clear motion), add captions, and use trending audio.
Can I create a baby dance from a photo alone? ▼ This tool also needs a motion video. Choose a sample or upload a reference. A prompt describing a dance does not supply the frame-by-frame movement required by this workflow.
How do I make a shorter dance video? ▼ Trim the motion reference before uploading, then check the detected duration and new credit estimate. The editor has no separate 5-second switch. You can also trim a finished result in a video editor.
Will a dance template keep every detail of my baby photo? ▼ No. Review the face, hair, outfit and body throughout the result. The blue-dress example shows that shoes and hair can change even when the dance remains readable. Simpler motion and a clearer photo can help, but do not guarantee exact preservation.
