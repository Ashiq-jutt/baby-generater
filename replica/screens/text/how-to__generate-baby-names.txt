Baby Name Generator
If you’re stuck choosing a baby name, start with broad preferences, then narrow down by vibe, origin, and meaning. This guide shows a simple workflow.
Use the baby name generator with your last name to build a shortlist you can actually compare. Start with the full family name, the languages your child will use and one or two priorities. Then check sound, spelling and meaning before asking for more ideas.

Give the generator a few clear constraints
Required: family name and direction Enter the surname as you intend to use it, including spaces or a hyphen if relevant. Choose Boy, Girl or Gender Neutral. These are naming preferences; the tool is not predicting a child’s sex.
Choose the naming culture explicitly Select the cultural background you want. The interface language alone does not choose it. For a bilingual family, select a main culture and put the other language and country in Additional Description. A shared Latin alphabet does not make English, Spanish and Portuguese pronunciation interchangeable.
Prioritize instead of overloading Start with a style and one meaningful constraint, such as a short given name beside a long surname. Add a preferred theme only if it matters. Conflicting requests such as extremely rare and immediately familiar can produce less useful suggestions.

When a culture in the list fits, choose it and put regional details in Additional Description. Check the sign-in, free allowance or credit cost shown before generating.
A short name with a longer surname
We will use the surname Montgomery in the United States. Suggest familiar given names with one or two syllables. No middle name is needed. Avoid a given name ending in the same sound as the surname begins. Explain the pronunciation and separate established meaning from a modern association. Choose the naming culture that fits your family and the Short style. Sound preferences are requests to review, not automatic pass/fail filters.
A name used in English and Spanish
The full surname will be García López. We need a given name that relatives can say in both Spanish and English. Keep the normal Spanish spelling and accents, explain likely pronunciation differences, and do not add an English middle name unless requested. Choose Spanish cultural background and add the countries where the name will be used. A name can work in two languages without sounding identical in both.
Read the explanation, then test the full name
Open a result Select a name card to read its meaning and why it fits. Origin, native writing and romanization may also appear when supplied. The explanation is a draft to check, not a dictionary citation or proof of popularity.
Say and write it in real contexts Read the complete name aloud as an introduction and as a school roll call. Write initials, likely nicknames and any middle name. Ask speakers of the languages you use about pronunciation rather than relying on a Latin-letter rendering alone.
Verify the claim that matters to you If a meaning or religious association determines your choice, look it up in a reputable name or language reference and note the source. For popularity, use the relevant country and year; a model’s “rare” label is not a current ranking.
Keep a small comparison sheet
Record the exact candidate Copy the full name, accents or original script, pronunciation, source for meaning and your own reaction into a note or spreadsheet. Mark uncertain meanings as “to verify.” The current results panel has no favorites or file-export control.
Refine one issue Save the current list before another run, because a new generation clears the visible results. Ask for one change: shorter spelling, fewer repeated sounds, a different style or no repeated candidates. State the names to avoid rather than assuming the tool remembers your shortlist.
Agree on finalists Reduce the list to five to ten candidates and compare them with your family. Keep separate notes for sound, meaning and personal preference. Before registration, check spelling and the current requirements with the relevant local registry.

FAQ
How many names should I generate before choosing? ▼ Aim for a shortlist of 5–10 candidates. It’s easier to decide after you compare a small set.
Can I generate names from a specific origin? ▼ Yes—set origin/style preferences and rerun to get more targeted suggestions.
What if I want a name with a specific meaning? ▼ Use meaning keywords (e.g., ‘strong’, ‘light’, ‘peace’) and iterate on the results.
Does changing the page language change the naming culture? ▼ Not by itself. Choose Cultural Background in the form and add the countries and languages you need in Additional Description. The International quick brief currently selects English, so review it before generating.
Are meanings, popularity and spelling guaranteed? ▼ No. Treat the explanations as suggestions. Verify important etymology and original spelling with a trusted source, popularity with country-specific data, and registration requirements with the relevant authority.
Can I save favorites or export the list? ▼ The current panel lets you open name details but has no favorites or file-export control. Copy your shortlist and explanations to your own notes before generating again or leaving the page.
