How to Make AI Baby Videos
This guide walks you through the simplest workflow: pick the right method, use a clear input (photo or prompt), set the right ratio, and generate. Follow the steps first—then reuse the worked examples below.
Start with the result you want: a new baby character, a moving version of a photo, or a baby delivering a line. The first two use Baby Video. A specific voice recording needs the talking or podcast workflow.

Choose the right starting point
Create a fictional baby scene Choose Text-to-Video when you have no source photo. Describe one baby, one setting and one action. The generated face can change between separate generations; save a still and use it as an image input if a later clip needs the same starting character.
Animate a baby photo Choose Image-to-Video when the photo already has the face, clothes and setting you want. Describe the movement instead of redesigning everything in the picture. An input photo guides appearance but cannot guarantee that every frame preserves it.
Make a baby say something Use a photo plus recorded or uploaded audio in Baby Talking or Baby Podcast. A smile or moving mouth in a general video is not proof of speech synchronized to your script.
Frame the photo for the movement
Choose the crop before spending credits. A face close-up and a full-body scene give the model different information.
Blink or smile Keep one sharp face, the whole head and some shoulders visible. Avoid a pacifier, hands across the mouth, heavy filters and a face cropped at the forehead.
Wave Include the shoulder, arm and hand, with space beside the body. A tight face crop asks the model to invent the missing arm.
Move toward the camera Use a wider composition showing the body and the surface beneath it. Leave room in the direction of travel. Inspect hands, legs and contact with the floor more carefully than in a portrait animation.
Choose the output shape Prepare a vertical photo for a vertical post when possible. Check how the editor uses the selected ratio; changing a square image to 9:16 may crop or invent content. Keep space for captions without covering the face.
Write a prompt you can actually evaluate
Use one main action and a simple camera instruction for the first attempt. These are starting prompts, not records of new generated results.
Avoid requests such as “walk, turn, clap, laugh and dance” in one short clip. To make a longer story, generate a few simple shots and assemble them afterward. Writing “4K” or “10 seconds” in the prompt does not replace the actual resolution and duration controls.
Photo → a small expression
The baby smiles softly and blinks once. The head stays mostly still. Keep the clothing and background consistent. Static camera. The photo supplies the character and setting. This version makes it easier to judge whether the face stays consistent before adding a wave or camera movement.
Text → a new scene
One baby in a pale yellow onesie sits on a play mat beside a window. The baby raises one hand in a small wave. Soft daylight, medium shot, static camera. Subject, setting, movement and framing are explicit. If the hand fails, retry the same scene with a smile before changing the outfit, camera and action together.
Set up a small first test
Model and duration Pick the input mode first, then a model that supports it. The current Vidu Q3 Turbo default is 5 seconds at 540p. Other models expose different choices; after switching models, recheck duration, resolution, ratio and the credit estimate.
Resolution Use a short, lower-resolution draft to judge movement before paying for a longer or larger version. A larger output cannot repair a blurred source face or a badly framed hand.
Sound Check whether the selected model exposes an audio option. Listen to the result before downloading. A request for laughter or speech in the prompt does not guarantee the sound or exact words you intended.

Review the full clip before exporting
Beginning, middle and end Play once at normal speed, then pause around the largest movement. Compare the eyes, mouth, number of fingers, clothing details and background edges. A good thumbnail can hide a poor final frame.
Keep a usable version Download a result you want to keep before trying another variation. Note the input, prompt and selected settings so you can understand what changed. Generating again is a new attempt and may use more credits.
Finish the post Trim pauses and assemble clips in a video editor. Add captions there, preview them on a phone and check that the face stays visible. Open the downloaded file and listen to its audio before posting.
Fix the specific problem you see
The face changes Try a clearer, front-facing source and reduce head turns. Keep the input photo and camera fixed while changing only the movement instruction.
Hands or legs distort Include the relevant body parts in the source. Reduce the reach, turn or number of actions. If you need a particular choreography, use a dance reference in the dedicated dance tool.
The background bends or drifts Try a static camera and a smaller movement. A moving camera plus a moving subject asks the model to rebuild more of the scene.
The words do not match Switch to a photo-and-audio workflow for a specific spoken message. Repeating the script in a general motion prompt does not create a reliable lip-sync workflow.

Tips
Keep motion simple (blink, smile, wave). Complex actions increase artifacts.
Tighten the crop so the face is larger, but include shoulders to reduce wobble.
Use clean backgrounds and good lighting for more realistic faces.
Change one variable per iteration (prompt OR crop OR ratio) to learn faster.
A motion prompt to start with: “The baby smiles and gives one small wave. The camera stays still.” Treat it as a starting point, then review the result.
FAQ
Which method is best for realistic results? ▼ Photo→Video is usually more consistent for realistic faces, especially when you start from a sharp baby photo.
What aspect ratio should I use? ▼ Use 9:16 for TikTok/Shorts. Use 16:9 for YouTube. Match your target platform from the start.
Why does the face look unstable or blurry? ▼ Most issues come from low-quality inputs or tight/incorrect crops. Use a clearer photo, keep the face centered, and reduce motion complexity.
Can I make an AI baby video without a photo? ▼ Yes. Use Text-to-Video to create a fictional baby scene from a description. Use Image-to-Video when you want a particular photo to guide the character and composition.
Can I make AI baby videos for free? ▼ Check the credits available in your account and the estimate before generating. This guide does not promise unlimited free generations; access and credit requirements are shown in the tool.
Can I turn an adult portrait into a baby in this workflow? ▼ This guide covers creating baby scenes and animating baby photos. For an adult-to-baby concept, prepare a suitable baby portrait first; uploading an adult portrait for motion alone does not guarantee an age transformation.
