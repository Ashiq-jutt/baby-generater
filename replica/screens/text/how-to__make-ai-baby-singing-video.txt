AI Baby Singing Video Generator
Turn any baby photo into a singing performance. Upload a clear baby photo, add an audio clip (your own or a sample), pick a model, and generate. The AI syncs lip movement to the audio for a natural-looking result.
Make the photo perform a recording you already have. This is a singing lip-sync workflow: it animates the face to your audio, but does not compose a song, translate lyrics or turn an adult singer’s voice into a baby voice.

Build the first attempt around one sung phrase
Choose the vocal moment Find a short phrase with one clear lead voice. A 10–15-second excerpt is a useful first test. Avoid a long instrumental introduction, overlapping singers or a backing track that buries the words.
Trim without cutting the word In an audio editor, leave a little room before the first syllable and after the last note. Remove long silence, not every breath. Play the exported file from start to finish and check that it is not distorted at its loudest point.
Keep the language in the recording The uploaded vocal determines the words, language and voice. For another language or a different singer, prepare another recording first. Changing the page language or writing translated lyrics in the prompt does not replace the audio.

Upload, select a model and check the estimate
Files and model limits Use one PNG, JPG or WebP photo up to 10 MB and an audio file up to 20 MB. MP3, WAV, M4A, AAC and OGG are accepted. Pruna AI Avatar, the default, accepts up to 180 seconds; Kling Avatar accepts up to 60 seconds. Switching models does not trim your audio.
Resolution and duration Both current choices offer 720p and 1080p. Start at 720p to inspect the mouth; higher resolution does not repair poor synchronization. Duration comes from the audio and is rounded up for the estimate. There is no separate duration or aspect-ratio selector.
Optional performance direction A short prompt such as “Gentle expression, slight head movement, steady camera” can describe the performance. It does not write lyrics or dictate a new melody. Check the detected duration, resolution and credits before generating.

Check the first syllable, held notes and final pause
Download a usable take and listen to the actual file. Add lyric captions, titles or a final greeting in a video editor; the singing editor has no subtitle switch. Check names and line breaks, keep text away from the mouth, and avoid doubling the vocal by adding the source track over an already audible result. The three archives do not include their separate original audio files, so they are not matched model comparisons.
The mouth starts too late First listen to the source: is there silence or a long breath before the vocal? Trim only what you do not need. If the recording itself loses the first syllable, re-record or choose a cleaner excerpt.
The mouth moves during instrumental sections Try a shorter phrase with clearer vocals and less accompaniment. Do not expect the model to identify every musical rest perfectly. Keep the same photo while changing the audio so you can compare timing.
The face changes on long notes Watch the eyes, jaw and cheeks through the held note and release. Try a sharper, more frontal photo or a shorter excerpt. Keep the audio unchanged when testing a new portrait.

Tips
Use a sharp, front-facing baby photo with clear facial features for best lip-sync.
Keep audio clips short (10–30 seconds) for the most natural results.
Test a short excerpt at 720p before generating a longer performance. Selecting 1080p changes the credit estimate; review it each time you change models or settings.
Avoid noisy audio or very fast singing—clean, moderate-tempo audio works best.
Add captions and trending hashtags to boost engagement on social platforms.
FAQ
What audio formats are supported? ▼ MP3, WAV, M4A, AAC and OGG are supported, up to 20 MB. The duration limit depends on the model: 180 seconds for Pruna AI Avatar and 60 seconds for Kling Avatar.
Which resolution should I choose for baby singing? ▼ The current editor offers 720p and 1080p. Compare the live credit estimate rather than using the old 480p prices shown in historical examples. The finished result also depends on the photo and audio quality.
Why does the lip-sync look off? ▼ Common causes: low-quality photo, face partially covered, or very fast audio. Use a clearer photo and slower, cleaner audio for better results.
Can I use my own music or just samples? ▼ Both. You can upload your own audio file or pick from the built-in sample clips (nursery rhymes, pop songs, etc.).
What photo works best for baby singing? ▼ A clear, well-lit, front-facing baby photo with visible mouth and no occlusion (no pacifiers, hands, or toys covering the face).
Can I type lyrics and have the tool create a song? ▼ This editor needs a recording. It does not compose a melody or turn typed lyrics into sung audio. Record your own singing or prepare an audio file first; the optional prompt describes the visual performance.
Will the singer automatically sound like a baby? ▼ No. The uploaded audio supplies the voice. Animating a baby photo does not convert an adult vocal into a baby voice. Prepare the sound you want before uploading it.
Can I make a baby sing in another language? ▼ Upload a recording sung in that language. The interface language does not translate the song. Review the actual syllables and mouth timing in a short test before using a longer track.
